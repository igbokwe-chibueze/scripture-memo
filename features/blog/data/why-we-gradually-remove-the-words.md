At the beginning of a waypoint, much of the verse remains visible. Later, the screen gives you less of its wording. The passage has not changed, so why does the task become more demanding?

Scripture Memo is designed to move from supported practice toward more independent recall. The words that remain on the screen help you begin. Gradually hiding more of them gives you opportunities to discover what you can now supply yourself.

We want help to be useful without making it a permanent substitute for remembering.

## Support gives you somewhere to start

Imagine being asked to enter a long passage before you have had a chance to study it. A completely blank screen could leave you without a manageable first step.

Visible wording, a word bank, and first-letter cues can make an exercise more approachable. They provide context or possible answers so that you can begin working with the passage.

There is no shame in needing that support. Early assistance is part of our design. The next question is how to create opportunities to practice with less of it.

If every answer remains visible throughout the journey, we cannot learn much about what you would produce without it. That is the practical reason for changing the amount of supplied wording.

## Glimmer: a supported beginning

Glimmer hides approximately 20–35% of the verse's words. Much of the passage remains available as context while you complete the five modes.

Use that context thoughtfully. Read the sentence around a gap, notice how a phrase develops, and examine the wording after you restore it. The visible text is part of the practice opportunity.

Our aim is to let you begin successfully while still asking you to do some of the reconstruction yourself.

## Glow: return with less visible wording

Glow becomes available twelve elapsed hours after Glimmer is completed. It hides approximately 40–60% of the words.

You are now working with the same passage after a delay, with less of it supplied. A phrase you previously completed with visible context may require more of your own recall.

That change can reveal a particular transition that needs attention. It can also show you which wording has become easier to supply. Both observations are useful for choosing how to approach the next attempt.

## Radiance: take greater responsibility for the passage

Radiance becomes available twelve elapsed hours after Glow is completed. It hides approximately 70–100% of the words.

Depending on the mode, you may still have a bank of words or another kind of support. More hidden text does not make every mode an identical test of unaided recall. It does mean that much more of the passage needs to be reconstructed.

Completing Radiance finishes the waypoint. It is a defined achievement, not a guarantee that the wording can never be forgotten.

## Why harder does not automatically mean better

Learning researchers distinguish immediate performance from more lasting learning. Soderstrom and Bjork's review explains why a smooth practice session is not a complete measure of what remains available later. [Read the review of learning and performance.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910388/)

That gives us a reason to look beyond how easy an exercise feels. It does not give us permission to make everything difficult and call the difficulty educational.

Our design needs to preserve a meaningful next step. Removing support should ask you to practice remembering, not leave you fighting unclear instructions or confusing controls.

If a gap fails to respond to your selected word, that is an interface problem. If an instruction is unclear, it needs improvement. Those obstacles are different from the effort involved in recalling a phrase.

## What to do when less support reveals a gap

Pause and identify the part you are uncertain about. Consider the surrounding sentence. Make a genuine attempt rather than assuming the first plausible word must be correct.

Where hints are available, use them when needed, examine the help, and reconnect it to the whole phrase. When feedback identifies an error, correct the wording and try again.

You do not need to interpret the harder attempt as evidence that the earlier stage was pointless. The tasks now differ. An answer that was easy to recognize may take more work to produce.

## Later Journey Stages change support again

Beyond Glimmer, Glow, and Radiance, the curriculum can return a verse at Learn, Recall, Strengthen, and Master waypoints.

Learn and Recall allow hints. Strengthen and Master do not. Learn is untimed, while the later Journey Stages have per-mode attempt limits of five, three, and two minutes respectively.

Those are current product conditions. They are not scientifically established best limits for every learner. We should continue evaluating whether the challenge supports useful practice rather than assume that less help or more speed is always better.

## Independence is the direction, not an accusation

Needing support at one point in the journey does not define your ability forever. The design gives you repeated opportunities to work with the same wording under changing conditions.

Our hope is that you can increasingly supply the passage yourself. Begin with the help available, attend carefully to the words, and use each return to discover the next part worth practicing.
