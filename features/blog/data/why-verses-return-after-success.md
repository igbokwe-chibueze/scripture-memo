You complete a verse, celebrate the waypoint, and move forward. Later, the same passage appears again. It can feel as though the game has forgotten what you already accomplished.

The return is intentional. Scripture Memo treats a successful first encounter as a milestone in a longer journey. We want familiar passages to become increasingly available with less support, including after you have spent time learning something else.

That is why a verse can return through Learn, Recall, Strengthen, and Master.

## One success answers one question

Completing a challenge tells you that you satisfied its requirements under the conditions of that attempt. It does not tell you exactly what you will recall next week, away from the word bank or surrounding text.

There is value in the success. You did the work, and the milestone should be acknowledged. There is also value in asking a later question: what remains available now?

The distinction helps explain why repetition after success is part of the design rather than a penalty for progressing. A return gives you another occasion to practice and another chance to notice where support is still needed.

## What successive relearning research contributes

Research on successive relearning examines learning to a criterion and then returning to relearn material in later sessions. Dunlosky and Rawson studied repeated relearning at weekly intervals and examined how it affected the importance of the initial learning criterion. [Read the study of successive relearning.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27027887/)

We draw a general design lesson from that direction of research: initial success should not be the only opportunity to work with a passage. Our Journey Stage sequence is our application of that idea, not a reproduction of the study's exact procedure or evidence that our particular curriculum is optimal.

## Two layers that are easy to confuse

Glimmer, Glow, and Radiance are the three challenge stages inside a waypoint. Learn, Recall, Strengthen, and Master describe the Journey Stage assigned to a waypoint.

A verse can therefore appear first at a Learn waypoint and later at a Recall waypoint. At each of those waypoints, you still complete Glimmer, Glow, and Radiance, using the five modes in order.

The first layer increases how much text is hidden across a waypoint's challenges. The second changes the conditions when the verse returns elsewhere in the wider journey.

You are not repeating an identical first encounter every time. You are returning to familiar material under the conditions associated with that later Journey Stage.

## What changes at each Journey Stage

### Learn: begin with room to practice

Learn provides hints and has no time limit per mode attempt. It is the supported introduction, with space to become familiar with the passage and the exercises.

### Recall: come back to a familiar passage

Recall permits hints and gives each mode attempt a five-minute limit. The verse has returned after earlier learning, and you have another opportunity to discover what you can recover.

### Strengthen: work without hints

Strengthen removes hints and gives each mode attempt a three-minute limit. The task asks for more independent performance within that condition.

### Master: reach a defined milestone

Master removes hints and gives each mode attempt a two-minute limit. It represents a defined stage in the game's progression. Additional Master appearances can be part of the curriculum, and review can remain useful afterward.

The timers are product rules. They do not measure spiritual maturity, and faster typing is not the same thing as deeper understanding. An expired attempt can be retried without undoing earlier completed modes.

## Why the later date is not identical for every player

Our curriculum currently schedules these appearances through waypoint order. Someone who progresses quickly will reach a later appearance sooner in elapsed time than someone who plays less often.

That means the game provides planned returns without currently supplying a personally optimized review interval for every verse. A future adaptive review system would be a further capability, not something we should imply is already present.

The useful promise we can make about the current structure is concrete: returning verses belong to the journey, and their later stages change the available support and timing conditions.

## How to approach a returning verse

Begin by noticing what you remember rather than assuming that you should remember everything. A familiar beginning may return immediately while a later phrase needs more work.

Read the surrounding sentence on the board, make a genuine attempt at the missing wording, and use permitted help when needed. When feedback reveals a difference, examine it before trying again.

Avoid interpreting a hesitation as the cancellation of your previous success. The earlier work remains part of your learning history. The current attempt shows what deserves attention now.

Where Study access is available, reconnect the memorized words with the wider passage. A returning verse is also a chance to renew your attention to its meaning.

## Why review still matters after Master

Master is a game milestone, not a guarantee that a human memory can never fade. The Vault gives you another place to revisit eligible verses, including mastered ones.

You may use that review without receiving new Beacon XP. The absence of a new progression reward does not make the practice pointless. A verse can remain worth remembering even when the next useful attempt does not increase your level.

Our aim is for progress to mean more than leaving passages behind. We want the journey to help you carry them forward. When a verse returns, the invitation is to remember it again with greater independence, correct what needs attention, and continue.
