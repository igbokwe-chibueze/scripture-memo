Should you use a hint as soon as a word is uncertain, or keep trying until you can solve everything alone?

Neither rule is a good description of what we want for every moment of practice. Scripture Memo includes help because learners sometimes need a next step. It also asks for recall because producing the wording yourself is part of the purpose.

The question is how to let assistance support remembering rather than replace every attempt at it.

## Leave a little room for your own recall

Before requesting help, pause and consider the surrounding phrase. See whether the missing word comes to mind. You may recall it fully, remember part of it, or discover that you need assistance.

The pause need not become a long struggle. It simply creates an opportunity to attempt the task before more information is supplied.

Retrieval research, including Karpicke and Roediger's vocabulary study, gives us reason to include attempts to recover information from memory alongside study. [Read the study of repeated retrieval.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/)

Our practical interpretation is to make an honest attempt, then use available support when it is needed. That recommendation is not evidence that withholding help indefinitely is better for every learner.

## Use help actively

A hint should lead your attention back to the passage. Once help is supplied, examine what it tells you. How does that wording connect to the rest of the sentence? What were you uncertain about before using it?

Continue with the exercise and notice whether you can now reconstruct the relevant phrase. On a later encounter, see what you can supply with less help.

This is a different intention from treating the hint button only as a way to make the current obstacle disappear. Finishing the board matters in the game, but remembering the passage is the larger goal.

You do not need to feel guilty about using a feature that the current Journey Stage permits. The helpful distinction is between assistance that gets you practicing again and assistance that becomes your first response to every missing word.

## Cue and hints are different

Cue is the fourth game mode, after Drag & Drop, Puzzle, and Swap, and before Fill. It provides first-letter support as part of the exercise.

The Hint System is separate. It supplies additional assistance where allowed by the Journey Stage and available balance. A first-letter cue in Cue mode is not another name for spending a hint.

Keeping those concepts separate makes the progression easier to understand. You can move through a supported game mode while still having a separate decision about whether to request additional help.

## Why hints are not available everywhere

Learn and Recall allow hints. Strengthen and Master do not. Those later Journey Stages are intended to ask for more independent performance when a verse returns in the curriculum.

That does not mean that learners who use hints are less capable or less committed. It means that the current stage has a particular set of conditions.

The support available also differs between modes. A word bank supplies possible answers; Cue supplies initial letters; Fill removes that first-letter support. Independent recall is something we are moving toward, not something we assume is already complete at the first waypoint.

Our exact support rules are product decisions. We should evaluate whether they help people practice effectively rather than treat the absence of assistance as an educational benefit in itself.

## A useful three-question check

Before requesting a hint, you can ask:

1. **Have I actually tried to recall this wording?** If not, make a brief attempt.
2. **Am I uncertain about the verse or about the controls?** An interface problem needs a different response from a missing word.
3. **What will I do with the help?** Plan to examine the wording and reconnect it to the phrase, not simply hurry past it.

After using help, ask one more question: what can I try to supply myself the next time this passage appears?

These are suggestions for thoughtful play. They are not additional rules that the game automatically records, and there is no scientifically established pause length that everyone must follow.

## What if you are still stuck?

Where help is available, use what the exercise permits and pay attention to feedback. If Study access is available, return to the text and its context. The game's study window opens before the challenge and reopens permanently after Radiance; it is closed during the challenge period.

That interface rule is not an instruction to avoid your Bible beyond the app. Your wider reading and reflection remain your own practice.

At a Journey Stage without hints, another attempt may be needed. A timed attempt that expires can be retried without undoing earlier completed modes. If the experience is being blocked by confusing controls, tell us about the product problem rather than assuming it says something about your memory.

## Help should lead toward greater independence

The reason to include assistance is to make meaningful practice possible. The reason to gradually reduce it is to create opportunities for you to do more of the remembering yourself.

Use hints as a step in that direction: attempt, receive help when needed, examine it, and return. The passage should become the thing you carry forward, rather than a screen you can only complete while every answer is supplied.
